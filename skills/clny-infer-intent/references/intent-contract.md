# Shared intent contract

Use this contract across inference, interviews, planning, and review. A small change can fit in a paragraph. Reuse an adequate existing document; do not create a second authority. An intent record may be embedded in the canonical plan. A product contract produced by an interview is one form of this record.

Include only what matters:

- Required observable behavior, scope, preserved behavior, and exclusions.
- Basis for that intent: current documentation, decisions, owner agreement, or explicitly qualified inference.
- Observed behavior and reported symptoms, with source and verification limits when relevant.
- Acceptance scenarios: concrete situations and expected outcomes.
- Firm constraints, conditional preferences with rationale, and delegated engineering choices.
- Consequential uncertainty, its dependent work, and the next authorized action.
- Canonical location and revision or source snapshot, decision status, and provenance sufficient for a fresh recipient.

Preserve 'I do not care' as delegation. An implementation convenience does not become a product promise. Keep conditional preferences conditional. A firm requirement may have uncertain feasibility; a verified capability need not be required. Do not erase a required outcome because it is expensive, or ask the owner to re-decide settled engineering discretion.

Distinguish observed facts, source inspection, reports, inferences, proposals, and decisions. Reading a test does not mean running it. Existing behavior is not automatically intended behavior. Product agreement, verified implementation, deployment, and customer recovery are different claims.

During exploration the record may live in chat. Before a fresh-agent handoff, persist it in an accessible, appropriate project location within authorization, or point to an already adequate canonical document. Check ignore rules and recipient access. If persistence is forbidden, label the chat proposal and identify the handoff limitation. Do not force a new file for a review that can cite adequate existing requirements. Reconcile revised intent before relying on older acceptance criteria.

## Documentation obligation

Every proposed or completed code change must explicitly review affected codebase documentation, including inline comments/docstrings, component guides, and relevant parent explanations. Identify the locations reviewed. Update inaccurate or materially incomplete explanations alongside the change. ADRs preserve history but do not replace the current account. Mark future behavior as proposed until implemented; do not claim deployment from source inspection.

A change is not ready while affected documentation contradicts or fails to explain required behavior or consequential constraints. If existing documentation is sufficient, record a brief, concrete no-edit rationale. Do not require cosmetic edits, a README per folder, or an unrelated handbook overhaul.
